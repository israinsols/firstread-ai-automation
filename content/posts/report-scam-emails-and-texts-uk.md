A text that claims a parcel is waiting, an email asking you to “verify” a bank account, or an unexpected message from a familiar name can all deserve a closer look. If a message feels suspicious, do not rush to reply or open its links. In the UK, there are official ways to report suspicious emails and texts, and the next step depends on what happened.

This guide covers where to report a message, how to handle it safely, and where to go if you already clicked a link or shared information. Reporting a message is not the same as reporting a financial loss; use the route that fits what happened to you.

## Before you report a suspicious message

Do not click a link, open an unexpected attachment, or reply to a message you are unsure about. Do not enter a password, bank detail or verification code through a link in an unexpected email or text. The [GOV.UK guidance on reporting internet scams and phishing](https://www.gov.uk/report-suspicious-emails-websites-phishing) warns people not to share private information or interact with suspicious messages.

If you are unsure whether a message is real, contact the organisation another way. Use a phone number, website or app you already trust, such as the contact details on an official statement. Do not use the contact information in the suspicious message to check whether the message is genuine.

## How to report a scam email in the UK

Forward a suspicious email to **report@phishing.gov.uk**. The GOV.UK service says the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) will investigate reported emails. Report it even if you are not certain: the official route is there for messages you think may be suspicious.

Do not click a link in the email just to copy its address, and do not download an attachment to inspect it. If you have already opened a message, avoid any further interaction. You can forward the email using your email app's forward function; do not add passwords or other sensitive details to your report.

If the email is on a work or school account, also follow your organisation's security reporting process. A workplace IT team may be able to check whether others received the same message.

## How to report a scam text message

Forward a suspicious text to **7726**. The official guidance says forwarding the message is free and reports it to your mobile phone provider, which can investigate the message. Most providers support the service.

The steps for forwarding depend on your phone. On an iPhone, press and hold the message and use the forward option. On Android, open the conversation and use the message's forward option; the menu may differ by device or messaging app. Enter **7726** as the recipient and send the forwarded message. If the short code does not work, contact your mobile provider and ask how it accepts reports.

Do not tap a delivery, payment or account link in the text to “check” it. If you are expecting a parcel or payment, visit the company's official app or type its known web address yourself.

## What if you clicked the link or shared details?

Stop interacting with the message and the website it opened. Close the page. If you entered a password, change it using the official site or app, not the link from the message. If you reused that password elsewhere, change it on those accounts too. Do not send anyone a one-time code or approve a login request that you did not start.

If you shared bank-card or account details, contact your bank through the number on your card or the bank's official app. If you installed an app or software at the sender's request, use a trusted device to get help from your organisation, device maker or a recognised support service. Do not call numbers shown in a suspicious pop-up.

If you lost money or your account was accessed, the reporting route depends on where you live in the UK. GOV.UK directs people in **England and Wales** to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/), online or by phone on **0300 123 2040**. In **Scotland**, contact [Police Scotland](https://www.scotland.police.uk/contact-us/). The official advice can change, so check the current route for your nation.

## How to recognise warning signs

A message may create urgency, threaten a fee, or promise a refund if you act immediately. It might use a sender name you recognise while asking you to follow an unfamiliar link. Spelling mistakes can be a clue, but a polished message is not proof that it is genuine.

Look at the request rather than trying to judge a message by its design. Be cautious if it asks you to:

- sign in through an unexpected link;
- pay an unexpected charge to receive a parcel or prize;
- share a password, bank details or a one-time verification code;
- download an attachment or install an app you were not expecting; or
- move a conversation away from the organisation's usual contact channel.

These are reasons to pause and verify independently, not a checklist that can prove whether a message is real. Scammers can change their approach, and genuine organisations may contact customers in different ways.

## Keep a record without engaging with the sender

If you need to explain what happened to a bank, provider, employer or police service, keep the relevant details. Note when the message arrived, the sender information shown on your device, and what you did after receiving it. Follow the reporting service's instructions about submitting the message.

Do not post screenshots publicly if they show your phone number, address, account information or a personal code. Do not forward the message to friends as a warning if it contains a live suspicious link; direct them to official advice instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do I forward a suspicious email in the UK?

Forward it to **report@phishing.gov.uk**. GOV.UK says the NCSC will investigate reports.

### What number do I text to report a scam message?

Forward the suspicious text to **7726**. The official GOV.UK guidance says the report goes to your mobile phone provider and the service is free.

### What should I do if I entered my password on a suspicious website?

Stop using the page and change the password from the service's official website or app. Change it anywhere else you reused it, and contact the affected organisation if you need help securing your account.

### Where do I report money lost to an online scam?

Use the official route for your nation. GOV.UK directs people in England and Wales to Report Fraud; in Scotland, report it to Police Scotland. Check the current official instructions for your situation.

## Use an official reporting route

For a suspicious email, forward it to **report@phishing.gov.uk**. For a suspicious text, forward it to **7726**. If money or account access is at risk, contact the relevant provider using a trusted channel and follow the current official guidance. You can also read the full [GOV.UK reporting advice](https://www.gov.uk/report-suspicious-emails-websites-phishing) and the [NCSC phishing guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-text-message).
